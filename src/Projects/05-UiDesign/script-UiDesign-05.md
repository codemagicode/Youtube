# UI Design: The Plan vs. The Reality

**Intro**
"Hello humans. Welcome to episode five of Hello-Kannada.
In the previous videos, we built the backend. It is logical, perfect, and completely imaginary.
Like a gym membership: very powerful, very unused.

It turns out users prefer apps with actual buttons instead of just reading raw JSON files.
Apparently 'Copy → Paste into Postman' is not a mainstream user journey. Who knew?

So today I wanted to design the UI. The plan was simple:
politely ask an AI to design the UI, and go get a coffee.

Unfortunately, the universe had other plans.
So today is a video about failure.
The *fun* kind. Not the 'production outage at 2AM' kind."

**Part 1: The AI Betrayal (Stitch & Figma AI)**
"First, I tried Google Stitch, the relatively new AI design tool.
I wrote a highly emotional prompt asking for a home page for our app…
including South Indian motifs for blessings from mummy and papa.

Because I don’t just want a UI.
I want **a UI that judges me lovingly**.

Stitch generated a nice UI, but then it betrayed me.
It refused to export an SVG.
It only spits out Figma files or raw HTML.

So basically it said:
'Here is your design. Now suffer. ❤️'

Fine. I pivoted to Figma AI.
I asked it for a simple layout of the home page.

Figma AI overreacted and built an entire, fully functioning React app.
Not a layout. Not a mock. A full app.
Like I asked for a glass of water and it built a dam.

If you remember, I am building an Angular app.

AI is supposed to take our jobs,
but right now it can't even follow simple instructions.
They were both fired.
How the turn tables!"

**Part 2: The Manual Labor (Inkscape)**
"Since the robots refused to cooperate, I had to do it myself.

A normal person would just use Figma.
But as we have clearly established, I like doing things the desperate way.
My tech stack is basically: ego + suffering.

Watch me open Inkscape.
It is a vector graphics tool, not a UI tool.
But I am desperate.
This is like making biryani using a toaster.

I am manually drawing rectangles.
Because nothing says 'modern frontend' like… rectangles made by hand.
As I am guessing what the color blue looks like,
I am surgically moving vector nodes to build the Home page.

At one point I moved a node by 0.2 pixels.
That’s not design.
That’s tax fraud.

It is tedious, yes.
But it makes me feel like I actually earned my title as a developer today.
Not 'Senior Developer'.
More like 'Senior Rectangle Engineer'."

**Part 3: The Penpot Struggle**
"Now, a static image is boring.
I needed to prototype the animations on these designs.

Since I refused to use Figma, I imported my Inkscape designs into Penpot,
an open-source alternative.

Penpot is great, but its animation system is… needy.

If I want a single button to get wider when you click it,
I cannot just tell the button to get wider.

No.
I have to create an entire, identical second screen
where the button is wide,
and link them together.

So to animate one button,
I basically have to create the multiverse.

It is like buying a second house just because you wanted to open a window in the first one.
And then the second house also needs a window.
And now you’re a real estate developer.
Congratulations."

**Part 4: The Developer Delusion**
"After fighting with AI exports,
drawing UI in a vector tool,
and duplicating screens just to make a button move,
I reached the final stage of developer grief:

delulu is the solulu.

I sat there, looking at my screen,
and I actually thought to myself:

'You know… building my own Figma clone wouldn’t be that hard… Right?
I could probably do it in a weekend.'

This was a dangerous thought.
This is how side-projects ruin lives.
This is how people end up on YouTube saying:
'So I accidentally built a startup.'

I had to step away from the keyboard
and drink some water.
And by water I mean… coping."

**Outro**
"So, to summarize my workflow:

I asked AI for help and got rejected like the proposal to my first crush.
I drew a website page in a logo creator.
And I almost accidentally started building a UI software company.

But we finally have a Home page prototype.
It is not perfect.
But it exists.
Which is already better than my backend’s personality.

Next time, we will design the chapters page…
assuming I don't lose my mind first with how busy I am with work already.

Like and subscribe.
See you in the next one."
